Why does a Xoloitzcuintli feel so warm?

A common belief says the Mexican hairless dog has a much higher body temperature than coated breeds. The sensation of touching bare skin is not, by itself, a clinical temperature measurement.

Common belief

“Xolos are constantly feverish or maintain a higher metabolic temperature to compensate for the lack of hair.”

Biological explanation

“Without a coat, the warmth of the skin is felt more directly, but body temperature still requires an appropriate clinical method.”

How to interpret the sensation of warmth

Distinguish between internal temperature, which requires an appropriate measurement, and the surface touched by the hand. The following visualizations are conceptual and do not represent clinical-study results.

Cultural context

Stories about the warmth of the Xolo

Historical sources and oral tradition connect closeness to a Xolo with warmth, companionship and symbolism. These cultural accounts are not therapeutic evidence.

Rheumatism and arthritis

Some accounts describe Xolos resting beside people in pain. This should be understood as tradition or an experience of companionship, not as treatment for arthritis or inflammation.

Guide to the underworld

In Mesoamerican tradition, the Xolo was associated with the journey toward Mictlán. Interpretations of color and warmth belong to historical and symbolic narratives, not veterinary evidence.

Asthma and colds

Oral tradition includes accounts involving asthma and colds. Closeness to a Xoloitzcuintli does not cure or extract disease and does not replace medical assessment or treatment.
