Tonalli Wallet and Tonalli Memo for the Xoloitzcuintli community: an easy guide from zero

If you came to Xolos Ramírez because you love Xoloitzcuintli and words like wallet, blockchain or bytes sound foreign, this guide is for you. You can start by simply reading the community. Everything else is optional.

The 20-second version: Tonalli Wallet is a digital wallet for using eCash and accessing ecosystem tools. Tonalli Memo is a compact public message board recorded on eCash. On Xolos Ramírez you can read a recent selection from the feed without installing anything or owning cryptocurrency.

First: what does this have to do with Xoloitzcuintli?

Xolos Ramírez did not begin as a cryptocurrency project. It grew around the Xoloitzcuintli, responsible raising, culture, families and lineage documentation. Over time, that community began using its own digital tools to preserve and share part of its memory.

Tonalli Memo is one of those tools. Instead of relying only on an outside social network to announce something, a short message can be associated with a public eCash transaction and later displayed in different places, including the feed you see on the Xolos Ramírez homepage.

You do not need to become a blockchain expert to benefit from it. If you only want updates, read. If you later want to use Tonalli Wallet or publish through an enabled identity, you can take the next step.

What can you do without a Wallet?

More than you may expect. The Xolos Ramírez homepage already contains a section called “Tonalli Memo · Our community’s living memory.” It reads the public indexed feed and displays a recent selection of posts.

  • You can read messages without creating a wallet.
  • You can open Tonalli Memo and explore the public feed.
  • You can identify the alias behind a post and inspect its public reference.
  • You can return later and see new activity.

The blockchain helps verify that a transaction exists and that the record satisfies the system’s rules. It does not mean the content of every message is automatically true. That distinction matters.

What if I want to enter Tonalli Wallet?

  1. Open Tonalli Wallet. It is a web application, and you can start at app.tonalli.cash.
  2. Create or recover your wallet. Your recovery phrase is the master key. Keep it out of chats, social networks, email and shared screenshots.
  3. Receive eCash (XEC). An on-chain post uses a transaction, so publishing requires enough funds for the operation and its fee.
  4. Set up your identity. The current Tonalli Memo composer requires an active, verified .xec alias. Publishing is also subject to Tonalli Memo’s current authorization rules.
  5. Open Memo. From the Wallet you can read the feed and, if your identity is enabled, open the composer.
  6. Review before publishing. Once sent to the network, the message is part of a public transaction. Treat every Memo as something that may remain visible.

“Bytes,” without the headache

Tonalli Memo is designed for short messages. It does not simply count characters because a letter, an accent or an emoji can use different amounts of data. That is why the Wallet shows a UTF-8 byte counter.

Current limit: the Tonalli Wallet composer allows up to 212 UTF-8 bytes of user text in a TM1 Memo without an attachment. If you add an NFT reference, some space is reserved for the attachment and less remains for text. You do not need to calculate it manually: the Wallet shows the counter.

Why not just use Facebook, Instagram or X?

You can keep using them. Tonalli Memo does not need to replace social media to be useful. Its role is different: it moves part of the community’s public signal closer to eCash-linked infrastructure, where a known post can be checked again through its transaction.

For Xolos Ramírez, that creates a second layer of memory. A photo can live on the website; a video can live on YouTube; a pedigree has its own documentary purpose; and a Memo can leave a short public reference that something was announced or shared.

What Tonalli Memo is not

  • It is not a truth machine.
  • It does not replace pedigrees, veterinary records or official documents.
  • It is not a place to publish phone numbers, home addresses, passwords, recovery phrases or private information.
  • You do not need to use it to belong to the Xolos Ramírez community.
  • Today it is not a mass social network with likes, followers or private messages.

So where should I start?

If you are visiting Xolos Ramírez, start with the simplest thing: read the feed. When a post interests you, open it. If having your own wallet and .xec identity becomes useful, then explore Tonalli Wallet.

That order matters. Technology should serve the community, not force the community to learn technology before it can participate.

Quick questions

Do I need XEC to read?

No. Reading the public feed does not require funds.

Do I need XEC to publish?

Yes, because an on-chain post uses an eCash transaction and must cover its cost. The current flow also requires a verified .xec identity and the applicable authorization.

Can I write a long post?

That is not the design. Tonalli Memo is for compact messages, and the Wallet’s byte counter shows how much space remains.

Is my recovery phrase published?

It should never be published or shared with anyone. Your recovery phrase and private keys are wallet secrets.

Community first

The Xoloitzcuintli has accompanied people for thousands of years without needing a blockchain. Tonalli Wallet and Tonalli Memo do not change that. They are contemporary tools for a community that already exists and wants to document part of its public activity with greater independence.

If the technology disappeared tomorrow, the Xolos, families and stories would remain. Tonalli Memo’s purpose is to help part of those stories and public announcements keep a verifiable digital reference today.
