🐾 Nutrition and individual metabolism

Coat variety, dentition, climate, activity and body condition can all affect how food is offered. There is no single diet for every Xoloitzcuintli: feeding decisions should be individualized with a veterinarian.

🦷

Dentition

Hairless Xolos may lack some teeth. Review the individual dog and adapt kibble size, texture or moisture with veterinary guidance.

🔥

Thermoregulation

Climate can influence energy use, but portions should consider activity, weight and body condition—not coat status alone.

🛡️

Individual sensitivities

Skin or digestive changes may have different causes. Do not assume an allergy or change proteins without assessment; elimination diets require veterinary supervision.

Needs by life stage

Requirements change as a dog grows. Select a stage for general feeding and weight-management points; portions and frequency must be adapted to the food, the individual Xolo and veterinary advice.

🍼

Growth and development

During growth, choose a complete puppy food and monitor development. Calcium and phosphorus should not be supplemented without professional direction.

  • ✓ Complete food formulated for growth and expected adult size.
  • ✓ Meals divided according to age, product guidance and veterinary advice.
  • ✓ Regular monitoring of weight, body condition and digestion.
🐕

Maintenance and skin support

In adulthood, focus on healthy body condition while observing skin, digestion, activity and dentition.

  • ✓ Complete food suited to activity and body condition.
  • ✓ Consistent schedules and measured portions.
  • ✓ Supplements only when a veterinarian identifies a need.
🛋️

Joint and digestive support

In senior dogs, activity, digestion and chewing ability may change. Clinical reviews help guide texture and energy density.

  • ✓ Adapted texture after reviewing teeth, gums and swallowing.
  • ✓ Joint supplements only when indicated.
  • ✓ Energy adjustment based on weight, activity and clinical findings.

⚠️ Foods that require attention

Some human foods can cause poisoning. Avoid offering them and contact a veterinarian immediately after ingestion or when symptoms appear.

🍫

Chocolate / cocoa

Contains theobromine and may cause vomiting, abnormal heart rhythms or seizures.
🧅

Onion and garlic

They may damage red blood cells and cause anemia; severity depends on amount and the individual dog.
🍇

Grapes and raisins

They may cause acute kidney injury at variable amounts and require immediate veterinary attention.
🥑

Avocado

Avoid the pit and skin; the flesh is high in fat and may cause digestive upset in some dogs.
