Why choose a puppy from Xolos Ramírez?

Choosing a Xoloitzcuintli should involve more than a photo or a size category. It also matters how the puppy was raised, what health follow-up it received, how it interacts with people and dogs, what documentation accompanies its history, and what support the family receives after delivery. Those elements are part of the Xolos Ramírez process from early raising through the transition to a new home.

1. You can meet the puppies as individuals

Public Xolos Ramírez profiles show photos, size, sex, color, observed personality, and current availability. The goal is for a family to meet a specific dog rather than choosing only a generic “Xoloitzcuintli.”

The current catalogue distinguishes available and reserved dogs so that a historical profile is not presented as current availability.

2. Socialization can be seen in real interactions

Early contact with people, other dogs, and everyday surroundings is part of raising. This does not guarantee identical adult temperament in every puppy; each dog develops individually. It does provide gradual experiences that can support confidence, curiosity, and social skills.

3. Health follow-up is documented

Puppies receive age-appropriate vaccination and deworming along with veterinary follow-up during raising. Xolos Ramírez has publicly documented vaccination sessions, allowing families to see part of the health process rather than relying only on a commercial claim.

Final documentation depends on the dog's age, individual record, and destination requirements. Microchip identification and travel-related health documentation are incorporated when applicable to the dog's process.

4. Identity and lineage are supported by records

Individual identity can be accompanied by FCM/FCI genealogical documentation when applicable, along with documentary records of the dog's history inside the Xolos Ramírez ecosystem. Digital records complement evidence; they do not replace official registration, genetic testing, or veterinary evaluation.

The useful question is not only “Does the puppy have a pedigree?” It is also worth checking which document applies to that specific dog, what information it contains, and how it connects to the dog's identity.

5. Families receive guidance before and after reservation

Before a reservation is formalized, the process can include a conversation about lifestyle, space, previous breed experience, other animals in the home, and delivery logistics. A video call helps answer questions and learn more about the puppy before arranging an in-person visit or payment.

After delivery, guidance can include adaptation, feeding, skin care, behavior, and everyday questions about living with a Xoloitzcuintli.

6. Delivery planning centers the dog's welfare

Xolos Ramírez is based in Mexico City and coordinates national and international delivery depending on destination. Timing also considers the puppy's maturity, weaning stage, health schedule, and applicable travel requirements.

When transport outside Mexico City is needed, logistics and travel documentation are handled separately from the dog's price.

7. Price and availability are published clearly

The current regular price of a Xolos Ramírez Xoloitzcuintli is MXN $42,000, with the same regular price regardless of size, sex, or color. There is also a special MXN $37,000 equivalent in eCash (XEC) option when the full payment is made in XEC; the exact XEC amount is calculated at the time of payment.

Delivery logistics are quoted separately when needed. See the complete Xolos Ramírez price guide.

8. You can review real stories after delivery

Family stories and delivery chronicles show what happens after a reservation. The site preserves testimonials and follow-up stories about Xolos already living with their families, including adaptation and delivery experiences inside and outside Mexico City.

So, why consider Xolos Ramírez?

Visible process

Profiles, photos, videos, and current availability status.

Documented raising

Early socialization and age-appropriate health follow-up during development.

Identity and lineage

Microchip and genealogical documentation when applicable, plus complementary documentary traceability.

Family guidance

Orientation before reservation and continued support for adaptation, feeding, skin care, and behavior questions.

The final choice should come from comparing individual dogs and breeding processes carefully. Xolos Ramírez publishes concrete information so families can make that choice with more context.
