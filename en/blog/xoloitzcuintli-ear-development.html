Anatomy of the outer ear

The outer ear contains cartilage, connective tissue, blood vessels, nerves and skin. Its form depends on structure, development and individual variation. The chart is conceptual and does not represent measured percentages in Xoloitzcuintli.

Components of ear tissue

🧬

Collagen

It forms part of the cartilage matrix and contributes to structure. It does not determine final ear position on its own.

💧

Extracellular matrix and water

They help maintain tissue mechanics and form part of normal function.

⚡

Elastic fibers and associated tissues

They contribute to flexibility and movement together with muscles, skin and connective tissue.

Qualitative observation stages

Genetics, variety and limits of evidence

Variants involving FOXI3 are associated with the hairless phenotype and dental changes. This does not support universal percentages for erect ears or attribution of ear position to a single gene.

What the evidence supports

Hairless variety

The hairless phenotype and certain dental changes are associated with FOXI3 variants. Ear position varies and should not be expressed as a universal probability.

Coated variety

The coated variety belongs to the breed. This guide provides no basis for assigning a specific percentage of dropped ears or inferior cartilage quality.

Reviewing common ear myths

Popular practices claim to “help” ears stand. Select each card to learn why these should not be treated as universal instructions and when professional guidance is appropriate.

🥛

Myth: extra calcium

Evidence-based note: Ear cartilage is not bone. Do not add calcium or other supplements to make ears stand without veterinary direction; unnecessary doses can be harmful.

🩹

Myth: constant taping

Evidence-based note: Improper taping can irritate skin, trap moisture or cause injury. It does not guarantee a final ear position and should only be considered with professional supervision.

Myth: shaving the ear
