Ancestral Touch: Xilonen Ramírez and the Xoloitzcuintli Bond

Fernando introduces Ancestral Touch with three-month-old miniature Xoloitzcuintli Xilonen Ramírez, exploring skin care, genetics, history and cultural memory.

Watch the full episode on YouTube

A connection that begins with a gentle touch

In a broadcast originally streamed live on TikTok, Fernando of Xolos Ramírez introduces Xilonen Ramírez, a three-month-old miniature Xoloitzcuintli puppy. Their encounter gives shape to Ancestral Touch (Tacto Ancestral), a program built around respectful contact, observation and companionship, alongside the breed's history.

Distinctive skin and everyday care

During the opening minutes (0:01–2:00), Fernando gently handles Xilonen while applying a balm made with beeswax and lavender, described in the episode as a way to moisturize and protect her skin. The routine becomes a conversation about trust and care. Skin sensitivities differ among dogs, so a veterinarian should be consulted before using homemade topical preparations, especially those containing fragrances or essential oils.

Genetics and a long history

From 3:11 to 5:03, the discussion explores the supple, smooth skin of hairless Xoloitzcuintli and its pigmentation. Fernando describes the role of melanin in skin darkening during sun exposure. Pigmentation does not eliminate the need to prevent sunburn or seek veterinary advice about sun safety.

Xólotl and cultural memory

At 6:03–7:40, Fernando discusses the breed's ancient history, referring to archaeological evidence dating back about 3,500 years. He also introduces FOXI3, the gene linked to the hairless trait and associated dental features, including the absence of some premolars.

Sizes and individual temperaments

Between 8:27 and 10:25, the program turns to the Xoloitzcuintli's connection with Xólotl in Nahua tradition and the symbolic role of the dog as a guide for souls on their journey to the underworld. This cultural thread connects the young Xilonen with Mexico's longstanding heritage.

A program for discovering each Xoloitzcuintli

At 12:15–12:49, Fernando presents the sizes discussed in the episode: miniature (25–36 cm), intermediate (36–45 cm) and standard (45–60 cm). Later (13:38–14:30), he challenges the assumption that small dogs are necessarily delicate and describes miniature Xolos he has encountered as confident and social. These are personal observations, not a rule about every dog's temperament.
